# Novelty Builder

## Purpose

`novelty-builder` constructs an explicit scientific contribution claim from a validated or appropriately reframed research gap.

Its central question is:

> Compared with the closest existing studies, what scientifically meaningful knowledge will this proposed study add that is not already adequately available?

The purpose is not to create impressive wording.

The purpose is to identify the **actual increment of scientific knowledge** generated by the proposed research.

Novelty must be:

- evidence-grounded;
- comparator-based;
- scientifically meaningful;
- proportionate;
- traceable;
- testable through the proposed study.

---

# Core Principle

Use:

> Novelty is a defensible difference that matters scientifically.

A difference is not sufficient by itself.

For example:

- new country;
- new software;
- one additional variable;
- new statistical technique;
- different sample;
- newer technology;

may represent a difference without representing meaningful novelty.

The novelty must explain:

> Why does this difference advance scientific understanding?

# Activation Conditions

Use when the researcher asks:

- "What is the novelty of my study?"
- "What is new about this research?"
- "How is my study different from previous studies?"
- "Help me formulate novelty."
- "What contribution can I claim?"
- "Can this study be considered novel?"
- "How should I position this study?"
- "What is the contribution beyond previous research?"

Use after the gap has been sufficiently validated.

# Novelty Is Comparator-Based

Every novelty claim should answer:

> Novel compared with what?

The relevant comparison is usually against:

- closest competitor studies;
- current best evidence;
- current standard methodology;
- current theory;
- current intervention;
- current validated model.

Do not compare only against weak or outdated studies.

---

# 1. Identify the Closest Competitors

Retrieve or preserve studies classified as:

- `VERY_CLOSE_COMPETITOR`
- `CLOSE_COMPETITOR`
- `PARTIAL_COMPETITOR`

Prioritize the studies most similar in:

- research question;
- population;
- theory;
- mechanism;
- intervention;
- design;
- measurement;
- outcome;
- analysis.

Novelty should survive comparison with the strongest competitor, not the easiest one.

# 3. Novelty Taxonomy

Possible novelty types include:

- `THEORETICAL_NOVELTY`
- `CONCEPTUAL_NOVELTY`
- `MECHANISTIC_NOVELTY`
- `EMPIRICAL_NOVELTY`
- `METHODOLOGICAL_NOVELTY`
- `MEASUREMENT_NOVELTY`
- `ANALYTICAL_NOVELTY`
- `VALIDATION_NOVELTY`
- `PREDICTIVE_NOVELTY`
- `INTERVENTION_NOVELTY`
- `IMPLEMENTATION_NOVELTY`
- `TRANSLATIONAL_NOVELTY`
- `CONTEXTUAL_NOVELTY`
- `INTEGRATIVE_NOVELTY`
- `TECHNOLOGICAL_NOVELTY`

Several types may coexist.

Do not assign multiple categories merely to make the study appear more innovative.

---

# 4. Theoretical Novelty

Use `THEORETICAL_NOVELTY` when the study may:

- extend theory;
- refine theory;
- challenge theory;
- specify boundary conditions;
- integrate competing theories;
- test a previously unresolved theoretical prediction.

Weak:

> Applying Theory X in another country.

Stronger:

> Testing whether the proposed mechanism explains why Theory X fails under a previously identified boundary condition.

---

# 5. Conceptual Novelty

Use `CONCEPTUAL_NOVELTY` when the study may:

- clarify construct boundaries;
- integrate fragmented concepts;
- redefine an inadequate construct;
- establish a new conceptual relationship.

Do not call terminology replacement conceptual novelty.

---

# 6. Mechanistic Novelty

Use `MECHANISTIC_NOVELTY` when the study moves beyond:

> Does X relate to Y?

toward:

> Through what scientifically justified process does X influence Y?

Mechanistic novelty requires grounding in:

- theory;
- prior evidence;
- biological plausibility;
- behavioral plausibility;
- social or organizational logic;

as appropriate.

Do not invent mediators.

---

# 7. Empirical Novelty

Use `EMPIRICAL_NOVELTY` when the proposed study will provide important evidence currently missing.

Examples may include:

- first strong longitudinal evidence after cross-sectional literature;
- independent evidence in a scientifically distinct population;
- empirical testing of an unresolved theoretical proposition.

Empirical novelty is stronger when it changes what the field can conclude.

---

# 8. Methodological Novelty

Use `METHODOLOGICAL_NOVELTY` only when the method enables an important scientific inference that previous approaches could not adequately provide.

Examples:

- stronger causal identification;
- prospective rather than cross-sectional design;
- multicenter design that addresses external validity;
- repeated measures capturing temporal dynamics.

Weak:

> This study uses SmartPLS instead of SPSS.

Software is not methodological novelty.

---

# 9. Measurement Novelty

Use `MEASUREMENT_NOVELTY` when the study:

- develops a genuinely needed measure;
- validates measurement in a scientifically meaningful context;
- improves measurement validity;
- introduces objective measurement where prior evidence depended on weak proxies.

Do not call minor questionnaire adaptation novelty automatically.

---

# 10. Analytical Novelty

Use cautiously.

Analytical novelty may exist when:

- new modeling resolves a genuine inferential problem;
- multimodal data require a new integration strategy;
- previous analytical approaches systematically failed to answer the question.

Do not use:

- SEM;
- PLS-SEM;
- machine learning;
- mediation;
- moderation;

as novelty by themselves.

---

# 11. Validation Novelty

Use `VALIDATION_NOVELTY` when the field has:

- developed a model;
- developed an intervention;
- proposed an instrument;
- reported a mechanism;

but lacks meaningful independent validation.

Possible forms:

- external validation;
- temporal validation;
- multicenter validation;
- cross-cultural validation;
- real-world validation.

Validation can be highly publishable without pretending to be first discovery.

---

# 12. Predictive Novelty

Use when the study advances from:

explanation

toward:

- robust prediction;
- external validation;
- calibration;
- individualized risk estimation;
- forecasting.

Do not equate significant predictors with predictive novelty.

---

# 13. Intervention Novelty

Use when the study translates sufficiently mature evidence into:

- a new intervention;
- a mechanism-targeted intervention;
- a substantially improved intervention strategy.

Intervention novelty should not be claimed when foundational evidence is too weak.

---

# 14. Implementation Novelty

Use when novelty lies in understanding:

- adoption;
- feasibility;
- fidelity;
- scalability;
- sustainability;
- implementation strategy.

This differs from proving intervention efficacy.

---

# 15. Translational Novelty

Use when research creates a defensible bridge from established evidence toward:

- clinical practice;
- education;
- technology;
- policy;
- industry;
- community application.

Translation must be evidence-maturity appropriate.

---

# 16. Contextual Novelty

Contextual novelty requires more than:

> a new place.

Use only when the context provides a scientifically meaningful boundary condition.

Examples:

- different health-system structure;
- meaningful cultural mechanism;
- different environmental exposure;
- regulatory structure;
- technology-access difference;
- institutionally distinct implementation conditions.

Weak geographic novelty should not be upgraded.

---

# 17. Integrative Novelty

Use when the research meaningfully integrates previously separate evidence streams.

Examples:

- genomic + clinical;
- behavioral + biological;
- quantitative + qualitative;
- education + learning analytics;
- material science + biological mechanism.

Integration must solve a scientific problem.

---

# 18. Technological Novelty

Technology can contribute novelty when it enables:

- new measurement;
- new intervention;
- previously impossible data collection;
- substantially improved scientific inference.

Do not call the use of AI novel merely because AI is used.

---

# 19. Novelty Level

Classify proposed novelty approximately as:

- `FUNDAMENTAL`
- `SUBSTANTIAL`
- `INCREMENTAL_BUT_MEANINGFUL`
- `LIMITED`
- `UNCLEAR`

Do not equate incremental contribution with weak research automatically.

Many valuable studies provide meaningful incremental advancement.

---

# 20. Discovery vs Validation Novelty

Distinguish:

## Discovery Novelty

Introduces previously unsupported knowledge.

## Validation Novelty

Determines whether an existing finding, model, measure, or intervention actually generalizes.

Both are scientifically valuable.

Do not disguise validation as discovery.

---

# 21. Novelty Architecture

A defensible novelty argument should contain:

```text
What is already known
        ↓
What remains unresolved
        ↓
What the closest studies already did
        ↓
What they did not resolve
        ↓
What this study will do differently
        ↓
Why that difference matters scientifically
        ↓
NOVEL CONTRIBUTION
